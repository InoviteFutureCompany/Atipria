Cookie Policy

Effective date: October 8, 2026

Last updated: October 8, 2026

1. Scope

This policy explains how Inovitefuture uses cookies and similar technologies on our website and any views embedded in them (together, the "Services"). It applies to everyone who uses them, wherever you are, and it sits alongside our Privacy Policy. Region-specific rights are in section 8.

2. What we mean by "cookies"

"Cookies" here covers every technology that stores or reads data on your device:

  • HTTP cookies — small text files set by us (first-party) or by a provider (third-party).

We do not use browser or canvas fingerprinting or CNAME cloaking to disguise third-party tracking as first-party.

3. Our legal basis for using them

Strictly necessary items are set because you asked for a service that cannot work without them (ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3) exemption; GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). Everything we currently set falls in that category, so there is nothing for you to consent to today. If we add anything else, we will ask you first and this policy will change before it loads. In the United States we treat your choices, including the Global Privacy Control signal, as an opt-out of sale and sharing under the CCPA/CPRA and comparable state laws.

We never pre-tick consent boxes, and "Reject all" is always as easy and as prominent as "Accept all".

6. Third parties and where the data goes

The technologies below are set by providers acting on our behalf, and they may combine what they collect here with data they already hold about you:

  • Firebase (Google LLC) — Sign-in, app data storage and push messaging. Processing location: United States. Privacy policy: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy
  • Auth.js / NextAuth (Self-hosted) — Signing people in with social or email accounts. Processing location: Your own infrastructure. Privacy policy: https://authjs.dev

Some of them process data outside your country — processing locations include United States and Your own infrastructure. Where that means a transfer out of the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, we rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, or the provider's certification under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

7. Managing and withdrawing consent

  • Nothing to switch off — we currently set only strictly necessary items, so there is no category to turn off.
  • Your browser — Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge all let you block or delete cookies. Blocking strictly necessary items will break sign-in.
  • Global Privacy Control — GPC is a signal your browser or an extension can send to ask us not to sell or share your data. We do not read it automatically yet, so please use the controls above.

Whenever we add a technology in a category that needs your permission, we ask before it loads and re-ask at least every 12 months.

8. United States disclosures

Under the CCPA as amended by the CPRA, and comparable laws in Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Texas and Florida:

  • We do not sell personal information for money.
  • We use no analytics or advertising technologies, so no "sale" or "sharing" takes place.
  • We do not knowingly sell or share the personal information of anyone under 16.

9. Retention and security

Lifespans are listed in section 5; none of our own non-essential items lasts longer than 12 months. Consent records are kept for 24 months as proof that consent was given, and are stored encrypted with access limited to the people who need it.

10. Changes

We update this policy when the technologies we use change. The date at the top shows the current version, and we ask for consent again when a change materially affects a category you had turned off.

12. Session Authentication Cookies

Strictly necessary session cookies are set for authentication via Firebase and NextAuth and are essential for account access.

13. Analytics and Behavioural Tracking Cookies

Product analytics and behavioural event tracking cookies record page and feature usage, errors and performance. These cookies are set by third‑party services and may be subject to consent where required.

14. Prior Consent In The EU And UK

For visitors in the EEA and the UK, no cookie or similar technology beyond the strictly necessary ones is set or read until you opt in, as the ePrivacy Directive requires. Consent is asked per category — analytics, marketing and preferences each have their own switch — and withdrawing it is as easy as giving it, from the same banner or settings link.

15. US Opt-Out Preference Signals

For visitors from US states with comprehensive privacy laws, we treat the Global Privacy Control signal and the choices made in our banner as an opt-out of the sale and sharing of personal information, including cookie-based advertising. Your choice is stored and applied to every later visit from the same browser.
